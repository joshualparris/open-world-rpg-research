# A Writers' Room for Every Repo: Voice Bibles, Naming Grammars and a Numbered Roadmap

**Prepared 2026-11-12 · Builds on [ORIGINALITY.md](ORIGINALITY.md) · Desk research plus measurements on your repos.**

Your idea: instead of (only) random draws, give each repo, game or world its **own writers and voice**, the way *Game of Thrones*, *Star Wars*, *The Hobbit* and *Doctor Who* each have a distinctive authorial identity with their own naming habits and quirks. Choose a set of original "writers", styles or frameworks per repo, and implement them one repo at a time.

---

## 1. The verdict

**You are right that this is better than random as the *primary* design, and it is not either/or.** The evidence supports a hybrid:

> **The voice defines the space. Code samples inside it. Writers draft within it. Validators gate it. A human is the showrunner.**

| What goes wrong | What random fixes | What a voice fixes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The model picks the same "safe" name every time | **Yes**: the choice leaves the model | Partly: it narrows the model's options |
| Names that sound unrelated to the world (a random Elara in a salt marsh) | No | **Yes**: a *naming grammar* makes names belong |
| Everyone talks the same | No | **Yes**, if the voice includes concrete speech patterns and examples |
| Quests all have the same shape | Only if premises are drawn from tables | **Yes**: structural habits per world |
| The world drifts and contradicts itself | No | **Yes**: a bible plus a continuity check |
| One person's tastes dominate | n/a | **Yes**: it makes the taste explicit and editable |

So the earlier recommendations stay (names drawn in code, banlist, registry, audit), but the **generator should be driven by each world's own naming grammar**, which is exactly what Tolkien did. I have upgraded the generator to do that (section 6).

**Honest limits.** No study directly compares "voice bible vs random draws" for game content, so this is a well-supported *hypothesis* to test, not a proven winner. The roadmap therefore starts with a pilot and a measurable pass/fail (section 7).

## 2. What real writers' rooms actually do

The useful question is not "what do these authors sound like?" but **"what mechanism gives each franchise its identity?"** Those mechanisms transfer; imitating a living author's sentences does not (and is an IP risk).

| Franchise | The mechanism you can copy | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tolkien (*The Hobbit*, *LotR*)** | **Languages first, then names, then stories.** Names are generated by one language's sounds and by systematic sound change between related languages (one root word becomes different forms in different tongues); each name fits a people's culture and character. He said nearly all his names come out of his languages. | [Philological basis of Middle-earth](https://web.sbu.edu/friedsam/inklings/Tolkien_Philological.htm) (general-interest source; check scholarship such as Shippey before citing) |
| **George R. R. Martin (*ASOIAF*/*GoT*)** | **History as flavour, not a copy.** Real conflicts and houses seed the feel, but he says he does not rewrite history "with the names changed". Names and factions are tied to a cultural/historical register. | [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/horrible-very-real-history-that-inspired-game-of-thrones) (secondary; the Valyrian naming system is not covered by what I found) |
| **George Lucas (*Star Wars*)** | **Myth structure plus borrowed real-world texture.** Campbell-style archetypes for the spine; names and places adapted from the real world (Tatooine from a town in southern Tunisia, where it was also filmed); a borrowed viewpoint (the story told from the two minor characters' side, from a Kurosawa film). | [Tatooine (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatooine); influences from [secondary pages](https://www.todayifoundout.com/index.php/2014/09/origins-jedi-star-wars-stuff/) (the Jedi etymology claim is weak; I did not rely on it) |
| **Steven Moffat (*Doctor Who*)** | **A tonal frame plus voice per character.** He pitched the show as a "dark fairy tale"; names that sound like a fairy tale (the show itself remarks that "Amy Pond" does); wordplay; each character speaks differently to each other. Critics also note the risk: clever gambits can outrun emotional engagement. | [Slice of SciFi](https://www.sliceofscifi.com/2010/05/19/moffat-doctor-who-is-a-dark-fairy-tale/), [on his wordplay](https://blog.commarts.wisc.edu/2010/07/26/words-are-cool-the-magic-of-moffats-doctor-who) (secondary) |
| **A TV writers' room** | **One final voice plus a continuity role.** A showrunner has the last say; a hierarchy runs the room; **story editors check that scripts follow the show's rules and continuity**; rooms range from two people to twenty. | [Final Draft](https://www.finaldraft.com/blog/simple-guide-to-the-tv-writers-room-hierarchy), [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/writers-room-definition) (the results did not describe a "series bible" in detail) |

**The five transferable mechanisms:** (1) a **sound system** that makes names belong, (2) a **tonal frame**, (3) **structural habits** (what a story does), (4) **distinct speech per character**, (5) **one accountable owner plus a continuity check**. A Voice Bible is just those five written down.

## 3. What the research says about "writers" for LLMs

| Finding | Source | Grade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Persona prompting raises lexical diversity** over no persona across 100 creative prompts, especially in **larger models**. **Coarse personas worked as well as fine-grained ones**, so you do not need huge character sheets. | Kambhatla et al., [arXiv 2505.17390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17390) | B |
| **Diverse AI personas preserved collective diversity** when people wrote with AI plot ideas (ten personas; ideas varied substantially). This is humans plus AI, not an LLM writing alone. | Wan and Kalman, [arXiv 2504.13868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13868) | B |
| **Hierarchical, staged generation** (logline → characters → beats → locations → dialogue) gave better long-range coherence than flat generation; 15 industry professionals co-wrote with it and could intervene at every stage. | Mirowski et al., [Dramatron, arXiv 2209.14958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14958) | A |
| **Specialised writing agents** under an orchestrator were **preferred by human readers** over end-to-end generation, and produced longer stories. | Huot et al., [Agents' Room, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02603) | A |
| **Asking a model to write "in the style of" an author** did **not** reliably match expert human imitators on *stylistic fidelity* in blind tests (lay readers saw no fidelity advantage, though they did rate the AI text higher for general writing quality); **fine-tuning on an author's full works** reversed that, and was far harder to detect (3% flagged vs 97% for prompting). So **named-author prompting is weak**; real style needs data or concrete constraints. | Chakrabarty et al., [arXiv 2510.13939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13939) (preprint; press accounts differ on the training-data scale) | B |
| **Persona collapse:** a model can look convincing one persona at a time, yet across large simulated populations many "different" personas fall into a small set of stereotyped patterns. | [Project page](https://ningshan-gif.github.io/project/persona-collapse) | C (project page, not peer-reviewed) |
| **Assistant personalities across labs are converging** ("Same Voice, Different Lab"); relevant because a "room" of LLM writers is usually one underlying model. | [arXiv 2605.02897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02897) (about assistant personality, not fiction) | B |
| **Long stories contradict their own facts, traits and rules**, which is why a persistent "story bible" and a checker pass are standard practice. | Microsoft Research, [*Lost in Stories*](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1163845); practitioner guides (vendor-heavy) | B/C |
| A **speaker "distinction score"** exists to measure whether generated utterances differ between speakers. | [arXiv 2405.10150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10150) | B |
| I found **no controlled study** that measures whether multi-character LLM dialogue is less distinct than human dialogue. | gap | n/a |

**What this means for the design:**

1. **Decompose into stages and roles** (Dramatron, Agents' Room). This is the best-supported part.
2. **Keep personas coarse, but give them concrete anchors** (a sound system, a lexicon, speech tics, sample passages). Persona names alone will collapse, both across personas and across models.
3. **Do not rely on "write like <famous author>".** It is weak evidence-wise, a rights risk, and produces pastiche (and, as your own repos show, generic fantasy names). Use **original, fictional house writers** defined by constraints.
4. **A room of LLM writers is one model in several hats.** Real diversity must come from *constraints, data and code*, not from hat names. Different models help less than you would hope.
5. **Always include a continuity role** (a validator plus a human), because that is what keeps a world coherent.

## 4. The design

### 4.1 The Voice Bible (one per repo)

A short document (about 1,500-2,500 tokens) with: premise and pillars, **the room**, house style, **naming grammar**, lexicon (preferred and banned), **how the cast speaks (with examples)**, structural habits, obsessions and taboos, 3-5 samples, a canon-change process, QA gates, and provenance. Template: [`templates/VOICE-BIBLE.md`](templates/VOICE-BIBLE.md). A machine-readable twin, `voice.json`, drives the generator: [`templates/voice.example.json`](templates/voice.example.json).

It is loaded as a **static prefix** and not rebuilt per turn, which matches your own rule of stable, deterministic prompt context.

### 4.2 The room (scales from 1 to 10)

| Size | Who | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | One *Voice writer* plus code | Small games: one-page bible, generator, banlist |
| **3** | Worldsmith (code), Voice writer, Continuity editor | Most repos |
| **6** | + Quest/plot writer, Companion/NPC writer, Contrarian | Lantern Road, Repository Town, AIDungeonMaster |
| **10** | + Lore keeper, Humorist/tone, Antagonist writer, Dialogue polish | Only where content volume justifies it |

The **showrunner is you**. Each writer is a *role with house rules*, not a pretend person, and is **original to that repo**.

### 4.3 Who does what (code vs model vs human)

| Task | Owner |
| --- | --- |
| Names, places, trades, quirks, wants | **Code** (`namegen.py --voice`) |
| Story premise and constraint | **Code** (tables) |
| Beats and scenes | LLM, one writer at a time, with the bible prefix |
| Dialogue | LLM *Voice writer*, with the 2 sample lines per tier |
| Cliché and slop pass | **Code finds it** (`slop_lint.py`, `name_audit.py`); an LLM *Editor* rewrites **only the listed spots** (external feedback, max 2 rounds) |
| Canon and registry check | **Code** plus an LLM check |
| Approval and canon changes | **Human** |

### 4.4 Day-to-day pipeline (Dramatron-style)

1. Showrunner picks a brief. 2. Code draws names, places and a premise (the **seed is logged**). 3. *Plot writer* drafts the beats. 4. *Voice writer* drafts scenes with the bible prefix and samples. 5. **Slop check:** `slop_lint.py` scores the draft; if it is above "ok", the *Editor* rewrites only the flagged spots (max 2 rounds), then re-lint. 6. *Continuity* checks the registry and canon. 7. **Human approves.** 8. Registry updated.

### 4.5 Naming grammar, the Tolkien-lite way

Pick **2-3 source flavours** (for example English dialect words, Welsh-like consonants, Norse compounds). Define: given-name **phonology** (sounds, length, endings); **surname pattern** (landscape/craft words, patronymics) with **one family = one surname**; place **compounds** (head + tail) where each tail has a meaning and each place a *reason to exist*; and, if you want depth, how names **change across regions or time** (sound change). Then the generator draws from that grammar, so names *belong* instead of merely being random.

### 4.6 Original writers, not imitations

Name your inspirations as **mechanics** ("history as flavour", "fairy-tale register"), never as "write like <author>". Do not reuse distinctive names or phrases from existing franchises. Canon-bound projects (such as the Kingkiller adventure) get **constraints only**: no invention, names come from canon.

## 5. Evidence from your own repos that this will help

- **Repository Town:** the cast audit found that "just 26 generated first names are reused 7-8 times each" across 188 house residents, i.e. a name-pool problem, not a model problem ([NPC realism audit](https://github.com/joshualparris/joshmemory/blob/main/repository-town/docs/NPC_REALISM_AUDIT_2026-10-19.md)). A per-district naming grammar plus a registry is the direct fix.
- **Lantern Road:** the **town names already follow a clear grammar** (Alderwatch, Blacksalt, Candlemere, Greyfen, Hearthwick: a landscape or craft word plus a function word). The **person names do not** (Edda Briar, Fen Lark, Yor Dane, Nera Vale, Tobin Reed, Alwen Reed, Tessa Reed, Mira, Oren, ...). "Reed" appears three times (this may be a family on purpose; worth confirming). The world already has a voice; it just was not extended to people.
- **AIDungeonMaster:** its own gaps list names "DM personality and continuity" as a missing piece and plans a canon log and persistent NPC records. A per-campaign Voice Bible is that piece.
- **Kingkiller adventure:** the plan already says "engine owns truth" and uses a major-NPC template; it needs **constraints**, not a generator, because the names come from canon.

(These are public repos. Private repos follow the same playbook but are not named here.)

## 7. How to know it works (A/B before you scale)

For the pilot, generate the same 20 NPCs and 6 premises in three arms:

| Arm | Setup |
| --- | --- |
| **A. Baseline** | The repo's current prompt; model picks names and premises |
| **B. Random only** | Generator without a voice file; banlist and registry |
| **C. Voice** | Voice Bible prefix, voice-driven generator, staged writers, validators |

Measure: **distinct-name ratio**, **banlist hit rate**, **surname repeats**, **a blind speaker-ID test** (can a reader tell three characters apart with names hidden), **human preference** (blind, you plus two others), and time cost. **Pass:** C beats A on all of the first three, wins the blind preference, and costs no more than about 1.5x the time. If it fails, fix the bible before moving to the next repo.

### The per-repo playbook (run for every repo in Phase B to E)

1. **Baseline:** run `name_audit.py`; record the numbers and 5 sample outputs.
2. **Choose the room size** (1 / 3 / 6 / 10).
3. **Write the Voice Bible** from the template; **derive from existing canon first**, and mark anything inferred as draft.
4. **Write `voice.json`** and **reserve existing canon** (people, places) so nothing collides.
5. **Add 2-3 human-approved samples** per cast tier (voice lives in examples).
6. **Wire it in:** names and premises from code; bible as the static prefix; the model is told names are facts.
7. **Add the gates:** `name_audit.py`, voice lint, registry check in CI.
8. **Pilot batch:** about 20 NPCs and 10 quests; **you review** every one.
9. **Measure** (section 7) and compare with the baseline; revise the bible once.
10. **Adopt and document:** commit the bible and registry; note the review date; add to the cross-repo index (item 24).

## 9. Risks and how the plan handles them

- **Persona names do not create diversity.** Mitigation: constraints, a naming grammar, a lexicon and human samples carry the voice, not labels.
- **One base model in several hats.** Mitigation: keep the room small; rely on code and tables for variety; measure.
- **Over-fitting to your taste.** Mitigation: the bible is explicit and editable; review quarterly; use blind tests.
- **Rights and imitation.** Mitigation: original house writers; inspirations as mechanics only; canon-bound repos get constraints.
- **Saves and stable keys.** Mitigation: names are display data; never change actor keys when renaming.
- **Effort creep.** Mitigation: the Gate after item 9; start each repo at the smallest room that works.
- **Evidence gaps.** There is no controlled study of this exact approach, and some sources here are secondary or search summaries (marked above). Treat the roadmap as an experiment with a stop condition.
